Free Mac app for DJs

Your Apple Music playlists, in rekordbox, using your own files.

CrateMate reads your Apple Music playlists, finds each song in your DJ folder by artist, title, version and length, and writes a playlist rekordbox imports. Your cues and beatgrids stay put.

The problem

Apple Music exports an empty playlist

You dig in Apple Music and play from rekordbox. Getting a playlist from one to the other should be easy. Apple Music can export a playlist as M3U, but only for songs stored on your Mac. If your library lives in iCloud, you get a header and nothing else.

Even when the files are local, they're Apple's copies, not the ones you've tagged, cued and gridded in rekordbox. Import those and you end up with doubles in your collection.

How it works

Three steps from playlist to crate

CrateMate doesn't use file paths from Apple Music. It matches on the music itself, so it works with cloud-only libraries.

  1. Choose your DJ folder

    Point CrateMate at the folder your rekordbox collection uses. The first scan reads every file's tags; after that it only reads what changed.

  2. Pick playlists and review

    Select playlists in the sidebar. Every song is marked matched, worth a check or not found, and you can fix any of them.

  3. Export and import

    Click Export to save an .m3u8 for each playlist, then in rekordbox choose File › Import › Import Playlist.

Features

Built for the way DJs keep music

  • Reads Apple Music directly

    Nothing to export first. Playlist folders and Smart Playlists are included, and iCloud libraries work.

  • Finds your copy

    Not Apple's file: the one you prepared for your sets, already in your rekordbox collection with its cues and grids.

  • Picks the right cut

    Track length tells an Extended Mix from an Original Mix or a Radio Edit with the same title.

  • Forgives messy tags

    Accents and non-Latin scripts, “&” or “and”, “feat.” in the title or the artist, typos, and untagged files named like 01 - Artist - Title.wav.

  • Flags duplicates

    If the same recording is in your DJ folder twice, CrateMate tells you, so the playlist points at the copy rekordbox knows.

  • Quick after the first scan

    Tags are cached, so later scans only read new or changed files.

It never touches your files

rekordbox keeps track of your music by where each file lives, so moving or renaming one breaks your cues. CrateMate only ever reads your DJ folder. Nothing is moved, renamed or retagged. All it writes are the playlist files you export.

Privacy

Everything stays on your Mac

CrateMate makes no network requests and collects no analytics. There's no account and nothing to sign in to.

What it reads

Your Apple Music library's song details, and the tags of the audio files in the DJ folder you choose.

What it writes

The .m3u8 files you export, and a tag cache in ~/Library/Application Support/CrateMate.

Questions

Before you dig in

Does it work if my Apple Music library is in iCloud?

Yes. That's what it's for. CrateMate reads the song details, not the files, and matches them against your DJ folder, so songs don't need to be downloaded.

Will I keep my cue points and beatgrids?

Yes, as long as the DJ folder in CrateMate is the one your rekordbox collection uses. The playlist points at files rekordbox already knows, so it links to the tracks you've prepared instead of adding new ones.

Does it download or buy music?

No. It only matches songs you already own as files. Songs it can't find are marked not found, so you know what to pick up.

What if it picks the wrong file?

Choose the right one from the row's menu, or search your DJ folder from the row. Close calls, like an Original against an Extended Mix, are flagged for a check.

Why does macOS say it can't verify the app?

CrateMate isn't notarized by Apple yet, so the first launch takes one extra click in System Settings. The install guide walks you through it.

Which Macs does it run on?

Any Mac with Apple silicon or Intel running macOS 14 Sonoma or later.

Does it work with Serato, Traktor or Engine DJ?

CrateMate writes standard .m3u8 playlists with full file paths, and it's built and tested for rekordbox. Other apps that import M3U may work too, but they aren't tested.

What does it cost?

Nothing. CrateMate is free and open source under the MIT licence.
