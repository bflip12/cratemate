How to get Apple Music playlists into rekordbox

On a Mac, keeping your cues and beatgrids. Published 31 October 2026.

A lot of DJs dig in Apple Music: it's where the new releases, the recommendations and the half-finished playlists for Friday live. The sets themselves get played from rekordbox. This guide covers the ways to get a playlist from one to the other, and where each one falls over.

What you actually want

Most DJs keep two copies of their music without thinking of it that way. There's the Apple Music library, often in iCloud, and there's a DJ folder of files you bought, tagged, cued and gridded in rekordbox.

So the goal isn't really “move the playlist”. It's: build a rekordbox playlist that points at the files already in your collection, in the order of the Apple Music playlist. Then every track arrives with its hot cues, memory cues and beatgrid, and nothing in your collection gets doubled.

Route 1: share the Library XML with rekordbox

rekordbox can show your Apple Music library in its browser, in a tree usually labelled iTunes or Apple Music. It reads this from an XML file that Apple Music writes.

  1. In Apple Music, open Settings › Files (called Advanced in some versions) and turn on Share Library XML with other applications. Or export one by hand with File › Library › Export Library….
  2. In rekordbox, open Preferences › View › Layout and turn on the iTunes / Apple Music tree.
  3. If you exported the XML by hand, point rekordbox at it under Preferences › Advanced › Database.
  4. Find your playlist in the tree and drag it into your rekordbox Playlists.

When it works, it's quick. The trouble is what the XML points at: Apple Music's own files, by their location on your Mac.

Route 2: export the playlist as M3U

  1. In Apple Music, select the playlist.
  2. Choose File › Library › Export Playlist… and set Format to M3U.
  3. In rekordbox, choose File › Import › Import Playlist and pick the file.

An M3U is just a list of file paths. Like the XML, they're the paths of Apple Music's copies.

Where the manual routes break

Cloud-only songs vanish

If your library syncs through iCloud, most songs aren't on your Mac as files. They have no path, so Apple Music leaves them out. A playlist of 40 streamed or matched songs can export as an M3U with only the #EXTM3U header and nothing else, and the XML tree shows them greyed out or missing. There's a separate article on the empty export.

You get Apple's copies, not your DJ files

Even for downloaded songs, both routes link to the file inside your Apple Music media folder. That's rarely the file you prepared: maybe it's the AAC from the store while your DJ copy is a WAV from Bandcamp, or a different master altogether. rekordbox treats it as a new track and adds it to your collection.

Your cues and grids don't come along

Because rekordbox sees a new file, it has none of your hot cues, memory cues, loops or beatgrid adjustments. You end up re-analysing and re-cueing tracks you've already prepared, or playing the wrong copy without noticing.

The wrong version slips in

Store downloads and DJ promos often differ: Radio Edit against Extended Mix, or an Original against a remix with the same title. Nothing in either route checks which cut you meant.

Downloaded tracks are protected

Songs downloaded from an Apple Music subscription are protected and can't be played in rekordbox anyway. Only music you own as files will do.

Route 3: match the playlist to your DJ files

The fix is to ignore Apple's file paths altogether and match each song to your own DJ folder by what it is: artist, title, version and length. That's what CrateMate does. It's a free, open-source Mac app, and it works with cloud-only libraries because it never needs the song to be downloaded.

1. Install CrateMate

Download the DMG, drag CrateMate into Applications, and open it. It isn't notarized yet, so the first launch takes one extra click; the install page shows where. Click Allow when it asks to access Apple Music.

2. Choose your DJ folder

Pick the same folder your rekordbox collection uses. This matters: rekordbox links a playlist entry to an existing track only if the path matches exactly. The first scan reads every file's tags, and later scans only read what changed.

3. Select your playlists

Your Apple Music playlists, folders and Smart Playlists are in the sidebar. Click one, or ⌘-click to select several.

4. Review the matches

Every song gets a mark:

  • Matched Found with confidence.
  • Check Probably right, but worth a look, such as a close call between an Original and an Extended Mix.
  • Not found Not in your DJ folder.

The Needs attention view shows only the songs that aren't a clean match. To fix a row, pick another candidate from its menu, use the search button to look through your DJ folder, or choose Skip to leave the song out.

5. Export

Click Export. Each playlist is saved as its own .m3u8 in ~/Music/CrateMate, with the full path of each of your DJ files. You can change the folder at the bottom of the sidebar.

6. Import into rekordbox

In rekordbox, choose File › Import › Import Playlist and pick the .m3u8, or drag it onto Playlists. Because every path points at a file already in your collection, the playlist links to those tracks, cues and grids included.

Tips for clean matches

  • Tag your DJ files. Artist and title tags give the best results. Untagged files named like 01 - Artist - Title.wav work too.
  • Keep versions in the title. “(Extended Mix)” or “(Joe Remix)” in the title tag helps tell cuts apart. Track length does the rest.
  • Watch for duplicates. If the same recording is in your folder twice, CrateMate flags it. Pick the copy rekordbox already has.
  • Use the not-found list as a shopping list. Anything marked not found is a song you don't own as a file yet.
  • If rekordbox adds new tracks instead of linking, the DJ folder in CrateMate isn't the one rekordbox points at. Check both, then export again.

Try it on your next playlist. Free, for Apple silicon and Intel Macs on macOS 14 or later.
